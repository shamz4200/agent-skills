# The $shamz Thumbnail Method

Build thumbnails that make a stranger understand enough of the story in about one second to become curious about what happens next. The thumbnail creates the visual question, the title adds context, and the opening of the video confirms the viewer clicked the right video. All three must tell the same story.

## The system: fixed template, one variable slot

Each channel gets a written thumbnail system file holding its fixed choices: one fixed typeface, one fixed colour pair, one fixed face-spot or corner accent. Only one variable slot changes per episode. Rotation without a system is fresh guesses that accumulate nothing; the system is what makes testing mean something, because one element moves at a time and the result is attributable.

- **Typeface:** one locked display face per channel, set in the channel's system file. Text style may shift with the emotional register (urgency = heavy condensed bar text; intimacy = handwritten script), but the lane is chosen, not improvised per thumbnail.
- **Palette:** a three-colour discipline per channel, roughly 60% dominant, 30% secondary, 10% accent. One warm accent colour (gold is the house accent metal) carries emphasis. Never hyper-neon.
- **Text colour:** sampled from the thumbnail's own image, never copied from a reference onto a different image. A reference is the principle only; the text must suit its own image.
- **Face-spot:** the fixed place in the frame for the face or product shot, so returning viewers recognise the next video before they read the channel name.

## The one-second test

Within about one second the viewer must understand subject + action or problem + emotion or stakes. Show enough to understand the situation, never enough to know the outcome. Curiosity comes from missing information, never false information: before the result, before disaster, a strange discovery, an unbelievable number, a confrontation, a transformation halfway done. The event must genuinely connect to the video.

- **One thumbnail = one idea.** One subject, one action, one emotion, one visual question. Secondary elements support but never compete. If removing an element does not make the thumbnail worse, remove it. Count the distinct visual elements at small size; more than three means delete until there is one story again.
- **Visual hierarchy:** main subject first, story object second (chart, money, enemy, rare item, massive number, broken equipment), context third (the background tells where it is happening).
- **Subject size:** the main subject takes roughly 30–50% of the frame when a face or character is central. Design TV-first: large subjects, large expressions, no tiny details. Shrink the thumbnail to 10–15%: if the story disappears, redesign.
- **Blur test:** blurred or squinted, you must still identify the main subject, the important object or threat, and the emotional relationship between them. If it becomes visual noise, simplify.
- **Contrast:** subject-to-background separation must read at roughly 7:1 or better (use the squint test as the proxy when no meter is available). Judge contrast against the feed row the thumbnail will sit in, not the canvas alone: paste it into a screenshot of a real browse row in the niche and check it separates from its neighbours, in both light and dark interface modes.
- **Faces:** large readable eyes, clear facial direction, strong silhouette. The character looks toward the important object (gaze steers the read: character → object → story) or straight at the viewer (gaze stops the thumb). Never blend both in one frame. Default expression is calm confidence; concerned or suspicious for genuine danger beats; shocked only for genuinely extreme in-video events. If the approved concept's expression is neutral or blank, ship a scene- or object-dominant thumbnail instead of a neutral face. If the eyes are too small to read where they point at small size, crop tighter (chin-to-brow) or switch to the object-led composition.
- **Bottom-right always clear.** Timestamps and interface cover it. Important information lives in the upper and central zones.
- **No deception, no mismatch.** Any element in the thumbnail must appear in the video. A real person or a real event is shown with a real photograph composited in, never an AI lookalike.

## Text gates

Default: no text unless it materially improves comprehension or curiosity.

- **1–4 words, one line, huge.** Text cap height must be at least 10% of frame height; if it shrinks below that, cut words until it fits. Maximum two text lines. Crop the text block alone and render it at 48 px height: if it cannot be read instantly, cut words or delete the text entirely.
- **Text completes the title, never repeats it.** Zero title-text overlap is a hard gate: the thumbnail and the title are two halves of one message. Thumbnail carries the concept or emotion; title carries the promise or mechanism. If the thumbnail text could headline ten other videos, it is too generic — name the precise payoff or mechanism instead.
- **Text is baked in at generation, never pasted on.** Generate the thumbnail as a whole image with the text rendered as part of it. Never paste flat text over a finished image.
- **Text style:** drop shadow plus stroke on every text overlay, gradient fill preferred over flat fill. One accent word may carry the accent colour; the rest stays in the locked text colour.
- **Second-person where the topic allows.** Direct address plus a stakes word ("YOUR FINAL TEST") talks to the viewer and opens a curiosity gap about their own world.
- **Titles:** every title carries an angle beyond the topic name, answering "why now / why should I care". Keep titles in a 55–70 character band with a hard ceiling at 100 characters. Prefer specific non-round figures ("$41,000", "300 seconds") over round ones, certainty verbs over hedging, and one number where the video truthfully supports it. No emojis in long-form titles.

## Generation workflow

1. **Understand the story:** premise, conflict, most surprising moment, emotional peak, payoff.
2. **Find the visual question:** the single image with the strongest curiosity gap.
3. **Sketch 5 genuinely different concepts** (different psychological hooks, not minor variations). Name one proven visual format from outside the niche and drop the content into it (three-tier comparison, triptych, tier list, countdown). Lay the concepts against the last three shipped thumbnails on the channel: if they repeat the same layout, one of the three bets must be a deliberate pattern-break (textless, classical-art, sandwich, split-composition, visual pun).
4. **Rank by clarity:** stranger test, small-size test, curiosity, title complement, accuracy.
5. **Build the top 3.** One locked title across all three variants; each variant tests a different hook axis (emotion vs curiosity vs proof vs process). Never change the thumbnail and the title in the same test run, or the result is unattributable. Keep one textless variant in the set where the image is strong enough — silence stands out in a loud feed.
6. **Simplify.** Then run the 5-minute micro-adjust pass before export: font weight or size, rounded vs sharp corners, add a frame or a single icon, tighten the crop, keep bottom-right clear.
7. **Mobile + TV test.** Inspect at full size and at phone size. Evaluate at small size, not just full size.
8. **Title pairing:** complementary titles, one per variant axis at most — the test fixes one title, so title variants are tested in a separate round after the thumbnail round resolves.

## Packaging-first rule

Thumbnail concepts lock at script stage, before narration or render spend. Five concepts sketched, top three selected. If no title pairs with any thumbnail sketch, the episode idea is reworked, never the packaging. The video is then built to match the packaging, not the other way round.

The packaging triangle is a hard gate: the thumbnail's claim, the title's hook, and the first spoken line of the video must be the same promise. If the best thumbnail concept changes late, reorder the cold open to match it — never ship a mismatch because the render is done.

## QC checklist (final, before presenting)

- [ ] Readable in one second: subject, action, emotion.
- [ ] Blur / squint test passes.
- [ ] Story survives at 10–15% scale and at phone size.
- [ ] Subject immediately visible; one story; emotion and stakes clear.
- [ ] Text (if any) is 1–4 words, completes the title, never repeats it.
- [ ] Nothing critical in the bottom-right.
- [ ] No clutter; no more than three distinct visual elements.
- [ ] No deception; every element appears in the video.
- [ ] First 30 seconds of the video validate the click.
- [ ] Character matches the episode's locked character sheet exactly (face, skin, hair, outfit, colours, proportions, signature attributes). Check the eyes on every thumbnail before presenting.
- [ ] Main thumbnail is a still that is in the episode. The other variants also come from episode stills, never standalone thumbnail-only images.
- [ ] Full-size zoom-inspect done: identity, anatomy, continuity, readable text, crop, focal hierarchy, premise accuracy. Fix mistakes before presenting.
- [ ] Feed-row test and dark-mode test pass; the story still reads with the bottom half covered.
- [ ] All variants exported at the same high resolution (minimum 1280×720 each); no low-resolution mock-up in the set.

Present three labelled concepts, recommend a main with a brief reason, suggest the other two for testing. The creator picks the main. Never replace or remake an approved thumbnail without approval; never upload, publish, or start a test without the go-ahead. If the last-episode thumbnails or the current character sheet are missing, ask for those exact references rather than inventing them.

## Testing the variants

Run one locked title with three thumbnail variants. Judge the winner by watch-time share — not raw click rate — after at least 1,500 impressions per variant, over about two weeks. Never read a verdict before the full run completes; early data reverses. Treat roughly a 10% or larger watch-time-share gap as a meaningful win; under 3% is no difference. Never hold publication for the test: if the test tool is unavailable, publish with the primary thumbnail and title and retry on the next upload. Never edit the title or thumbnail by hand while a test runs — that stops the experiment and it must restart. Never change the thumbnail of a video inside an active test window or an active distribution push.

When a test resolves, extract the winner's DNA (expression, text length, composition, palette) and lock it as that channel's default concept A for the next episode. New variants must beat the old winner, not beat each other from scratch. Log every variant and outcome in the channel's thumbnail performance log (video, concept, title, topic, emotion, pattern, text or no-text, face or no-face, impressions, click rate, average view duration, retention, traffic source, early and final reads). Judge click rate against the channel's own baseline and segmented by traffic source, never as a single blended number.

Test in impact order: contrast first, then differentiated colour, expressive face, short text, warm-vs-cool temperature. Never burn two of the three bets on the same rung.

## Portrait vs landscape

- **Long-form (landscape 16:9):** generate at 1280×720 or higher; export test variants at the highest resolution the platform accepts. All rules above are written for this surface.
- **Vertical Shorts / teasers (9:16):** design for the vertical surface from the start — never crop a landscape thumbnail into a portrait slot. Keep the subject and any text inside the vertical safe zones, with the same one-idea, bottom-right-clear, and text-gate rules applied to the portrait frame.

## Channel-parameter example (wisdom / philosophy lane)

Parameters, not a second skill: a wisdom lane may set its system file to a distressed vintage slab-serif type lane, a near-black plus gold palette lock with one neon accent per episode, a split composition (imagery on the left two-thirds, solid text panel on the right third), classical or esoteric art mixed with the channel character, second-person urgency text, and a solitary figure in vast symbolic space. The gates above do not change; only the fixed template values do.

## What this skill does not do

- It does not generate the video, write the script, or pick the title angle for you — it gates the packaging.
- It does not publish, upload, or start a test on its own. Every test starts on the creator's say-so.
