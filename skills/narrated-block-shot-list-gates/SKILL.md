# Narrated Block Shot List Gates

## Measure the narration rate first

- Measure the delivered narration rate from a timed transcript before you write duration estimates.
- Record the measured words per minute and the source file in the shot list header.
- Never use an assumed rate for an estimate.
- Recalculate every shot duration from the measured rate and the timed words for that shot.
- Report a mismatch between the expected block length and the measured length before you build, and do not hide the mismatch by padding.

## Keep narration story only

- Write narrator text as story, feeling, decision, or consequence only.
- Keep motion, hand, arm, step, pose, and camera instructions in the verb and camera columns only.
- Scan the full narrator text for repeated motion words before you record any narration.
- Rewrite any narrator line that exists only to describe a hand, arm, step, or pose.
- Treat a repeated motion phrase across many shots as a failure of the list, not as a style choice.

## Vary camera angles on the list

- Add a camera angle column to every shot in the list.
- Choose the angle for the story beat, using over the shoulder, three quarter, side profile, behind, high, low, wide, or close detail.
- Use a front facing angle only when the story needs a direct face, a reaction, or a direct address.
- Count front facing shots and publish the count with the total before any stills are made.
- Fail the list when front facing shots dominate it or when neighbouring shots repeat the same angle.

## Gate the list before production

- Check the list for shot count, word count, duration, presence split, and angle count before you generate a still.
- Never floor a shot duration to a pacing band and never pad narration with silence to reach a target length.
- Let shot durations follow the timed words for each shot, and treat any pacing band as a check only.
- Scan the delivered segment for voice gaps, and rebuild any shot boundary gap over about one second that is not a deliberate pause.
- Present the shot list for a verdict before you generate stills, record narration, or build a segment.
- Do not start a later block until the fixed block passes its flow verdict.

## Alternatives for better results

- Use the character sheet quality control method after the list passes, to inspect each still individually against its locked sheet.
- Use the segment audio hygiene method after narration is recorded, to assemble chunks without gaps, drift, or bursts.
