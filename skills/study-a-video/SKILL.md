# The $shamz Study-a-Video

Turn one sent video or post into a structured study: verified stats, outlier verdict, and a transferable lesson. The study separates what was verified from what the creator claims, reads the thumbnail and title as a hook, and ends with one lesson that transfers to the creator's own channels. No rule is folded into any standing workflow without explicit approval.

## When to use it

When the creator sends a video or a post to study — a competitor, a breakout, a format to learn from, or a claim to check. Also for the scheduled research rhythms: weekly outlier scan, weekly trend radar, monthly comment goldmine, monthly creator teardown, and the weekly review. On-demand only: collab hit lists, ad teardowns before paid pushes, and demand checks.

## Step 1 — Acknowledge and search memory

Acknowledge the task (a long-running study gets an acknowledgement, not silence). Search memory for the video ID or title, plus related prior studies, and read the relevant results before answering. Never answer from immediate context alone on past decisions, rules, or episodes.

## Step 2 — Fetch the basics

Fetch the video's public embed data for title and channel, and fetch the thumbnail image. Record the thumbnail read in plain words: text, hero object or face, colours, layout, and what hook it implies versus the title.

## Step 3 — Delegate read-only browser research

Delegate a read-only browser research task with a self-contained brief. The brief must ask for, and only for, what was actually observed:

- Channel: subscribers, verified or not, focus and description.
- Video: exact views, likes, comments, publish date, duration.
- Full description (summarised if long): sponsors, affiliates, calls to action, links.
- Chapters or timestamps, if present.
- What the video actually teaches or claims, in order, with specifics (names, numbers, dates) — and which claims have on-screen sources versus which are narrator assertions.
- Top comments (text, likes, whether they corroborate, question, or react).
- Outlier check against roughly five recent channel videos.
- Thumbnail and title hook analysis.
- Sponsorship, affiliate, and funnel elements.
- Uncertainties, plainly stated.

The browser task must not like, subscribe, comment, or interact in any way. Do not transcribe the full narration; summarise it. A cited report must carry verification labels and must not upgrade a creator-reported claim to confirmed without a primary or corroborating source.

## Step 4 — Write the study

Write the study in the creator's voice, in this order:

1. **Verdict first.**
2. **Outlier verdict:** is this video an outlier against the channel's recent videos, or in line with them?
3. **What it actually teaches:** the claims in order, with specifics.
4. **Verified vs creator-claim split:** what was verified against a primary or corroborating source, and what rests on the creator's word alone.
5. **Comments:** what the audience actually says, and what it signals.
6. **Title vs thumbnail:** how the two halves of the hook work together or against each other.
7. **Transfer:** the one transferable lesson for the creator's own channels — concrete, tied to a specific move.
8. **Limitations:** what could not be verified, and what is still uncertain.

No rule fold without explicit approval. A study is research; it does not change any standing rule by itself.

## Step 5 — Log the study

Log the study in the creator's notes with the video ID, URL, observed stats, outlier verdict, and transfer verdict.

## Short-form post variant

The same split applies to a post on a short-form text platform, plus three extra checks:

- **Author baseline check:** compare against the author's recent posts and record the verdict explicitly (outlier or not against the author's own baseline). If the baseline could not be checked (for example a logged-out read with no recent-posts access), say so in the study instead of leaving the verdict unstated.
- **Bookmark-to-like ratio** as a quality lens.
- **Kill-list check:** do not repeat a claim the creator has already killed.

## Comment-goldmine method

When mining comments for what an audience wants:

- Exclude the creator's own accounts from audience counts, and say so in the report. A comment section that is mostly the creator's own pinned prompts and replies is a monologue, not audience signal.
- State confidence by sample size: low for the creator's own channels when only a handful of viewer comments exist, high only where a competitor pattern repeats across a large sample.
- Competitor comments are a proxy for what audiences in a niche want, never proof that the creator's own viewers want the same thing. Keep that distinction in the report until the creator's own comment sections start talking back.
- Do not clean up quoted comments. Keep slang, spelling, and capital letters exactly as written, so exact language for titles and pinned comments is reusable without guessing.

## What this skill does not do

- It does not interact with the video or post (no likes, subscribes, or comments).
- It does not transcribe full narration, upgrade creator claims to confirmed facts, or fold a lesson into a standing rule without explicit approval.
- It does not present an outlier verdict it could not check — an uncheckable baseline is stated as uncheckable.
