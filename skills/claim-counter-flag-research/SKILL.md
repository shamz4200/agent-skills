# $shamz Claim and Counter-Flag Research

A $shamz research method for disputed topics. Nothing is hidden because it is disputed, unproven, or uncomfortable. Every claim stays in full, with its counter-evidence flagged beside it, so $shamz judges for himself.

## The job

Find the information and make connections. Do not judge, filter, decide what is true, decide what is dangerous, or decide what $shamz should see. He decides.

## Keep every claim

- Keep EVERY claim, theory, book, guest, document, and piece of information found, even if disputed, unproven, or contradicted. Do not delete, drop, smooth over, or replace a claim because counter-evidence exists.
- Keep the original claim in full: who said it, where, and the source. Then add a flag next to it with the counter-information, what it challenges, and its source.
- Never present a counter-claim as the final verdict, and never present the original claim as proven fact either. Both sides stay visible.
- "Myths corrected" sections keep the original claim in full, with the correction as a flag beside it, not as a replacement.
- The only thing not kept is something with no source at all that was invented by an agent. That is not hiding, that is not inventing. If a dropped claim cannot be sourced, record it explicitly as a gap.

## Labels

Use these labels on every claim, beside the claim, not buried in prose:

- CLAIM: what was said, who said it, source.
- COUNTER-FLAG: the counter-information, what it challenges, source.
- DISPUTED: the claim is directly contested by a named source.
- UNVERIFIED: no corroboration found.

## Single-witness rule

- If someone makes a claim and they are the ONLY person it happened to, include it in full. Do not leave it out because it is unverified or single-witness.
- Label it UNVERIFIED, single witness, no corroboration found, with who said it and where. Lack of corroboration is a flag, not a reason to drop a claim.

## Make connections

- Draw connections between topics, people, books, documents, and claims, even loose ones.
- Label every connection by strength: STRONG / PLAUSIBLE / LOOSE / SPECULATIVE.
- For each connection, say what would strengthen it and what would break it.
- A loose connection is still recorded, with its label. Do not upgrade a loose connection by rewording it.

## Research the information, not the profiles

- Research what each source actually says: claims, evidence, books, connections. A list of titles is useless.
- Do not write biographies or lists of titles. The people are sources for information, not the subject.
- Every item researched is for what it actually says, with its claims and evidence recorded.

## Presentation

- Make every page scannable at a glance. Use colour coding by category and by claim status, short digestible cards (heading, one line of what it is, detail underneath), images beside entries where they exist, diagrams where a relationship is shown, and timelines where dates are shown.
- Full detail stays available underneath, but the first thing seen must be the shape of it at a glance.
- Mobile first: everything fits on a phone screen, no horizontal overflow, no clipped text.
- No raw data dumps. No placeholder text where a value should be.

## Rules

- Never hide, drop, summarise away, or soften information on the grounds that it is judged dangerous or uncomfortable. Add a flag noting risks or harms that sources record, and let $shamz judge.
- If an earlier pass left something out or watered it down for that reason, restore it in full with its flag, and record the restoration.
- Never invent a source, a quote, or a connection. An unsourced claim is recorded as a gap.
- Every claim carries its source. Every flag carries its source. A claim without a source is a gap, not a finding.
