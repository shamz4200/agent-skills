# Narrated Block Shot List

## Measure the narration rate first

- Measure the delivered narration rate from a timed transcript before you write duration estimates.

- Record the measured words per minute and the source file in the shot list header.

- Never use an assumed rate for an estimate.

- Recalculate every shot duration from the measured rate and the timed words for that shot.

- Report a mismatch between the expected block length and the measured length before you build, and do not hide the mismatch by padding.

## Keep narration story only and match every shot to its narration

- Write narrator text as story, feeling, decision, or consequence only.

- Keep motion, hand, arm, step, pose, and camera instructions in the verb and camera columns only.

- Rewrite any narrator line that exists only to describe a hand, arm, step, or pose.

- Read the narrator text for one shot and name every character it mentions or implies before you choose who appears in that shot.

- Show the character who is named or speaking in that shot, doing the action or holding the object the narrator text describes.

- Never show a different character in place of the named one, and never show an empty room or object when the narrator text describes a character acting or speaking.

- Use an environment only shot only when the narrator text describes a place or object with no character action in it.

- Run a mute test on every shot with its narrator text beside it, and fail any shot whose story is unclear with the sound off.

- Scan the full narrator text for repeated motion words before you record any narration, and treat a repeated motion phrase across many shots as a failure of the list.

## Vary camera angles and lock one location with the group together

- Add a camera angle column to every shot in the list.

- Choose the angle for the story beat, using over the shoulder, three quarter, side profile, behind, high, low, wide, or close detail.

- Use a front facing angle only when the story needs a direct face, a reaction, or a direct address.

- Count front facing shots and publish the count with the total before any stills are made.

- Fail the list when front facing shots dominate it or when neighbouring shots repeat the same angle.

- Name one primary location for the block in the shot list header before any still is made.

- Keep every interior shot in that same location unless the narrator text says the scene has moved, and name the move in the shot where it happens.

- Count group shots for a scene the story tells as a group, and require shots with two or more characters together where the characters are together in the story.

- Treat a reflection as the character in frame, and treat a character inside a framed photo as environment rather than as the character directly in frame.

- Never let a block drift across unrelated rooms or streets while the narration stays in one room.

## Keep every verb distinct

- Give every shot its own verb that depicts that shot's specific action, object, or place.

- Count distinct verbs across the whole list, and fail the list when any verb repeats.

- Never reuse a generic pose or listening verb across many shots to save time, because repeated verbs produce repeated stills even when the compositions differ.

- Rewrite a shot whose verb could fit any other shot until its verb names only that shot.

## Gate the list before production

- Check the list for shot count, word count, duration, presence split, and angle count before you generate a still.

- Never floor a shot duration to a pacing band and never pad narration with silence to reach a target length.

- Let shot durations follow the timed words for each shot, and treat any pacing band as a check only.

- Publish a match scan in the shot list header before any stills or narration are made, with group shot count, location lock name, distinct verb count, and who mismatch count.

- Read the whole list shot by shot yourself, and record that you did so, rather than relying on a worker report alone.

- Fail the list when the scan shows any who mismatch, any recycled verb, zero group shots in a group scene, or no location lock.

- Rewrite the failing list and rescan it to zero before stills begin, and regenerate any still that was built from a failing list.

- Scan the delivered segment for voice gaps, and rebuild any shot boundary gap over about one second that is not a deliberate pause.

- Check each still against its sheet and against its own narrator text, and reject a still that matches the sheet but shows the wrong character for its line.

- Watch the assembled segment with the shot list in hand, and check that each shot still shows its own narration at its own timestamp.

- Present the passed list with its scan for a verdict before any stills, narration, or segment work starts.

- Do not start a later block until the fixed block passes its flow verdict.
