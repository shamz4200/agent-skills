# Glow Study 60 s test: "Nobody Saw It" (the spotlight effect)

Ending: complete (closes the thread, a question for the comments, subscribe).
Format: second person ("you"), Shamz is the "you" on screen. One study, cited on screen.

[SRC] Gilovich, Medvec & Savitsky (2000), "The spotlight effect in social judgment", Journal of Personality and
Social Psychology 78(2), 211-222. Cornell students wore an embarrassing T-shirt into a room of other students; they
guessed about half the room would remember it; about a quarter (23%) did.

## Test
You trip on the stairs at school.
Books everywhere.
Fifty people saw it.
At least, that's what you think.
Tonight, at two in the morning, you replay it.
Again.
And again.
But here's the thing.
In the year two thousand, researchers at Cornell University ran a test.
They sent students into a room full of people, wearing an embarrassing T-shirt.
Then they asked them: how many people noticed?
The students guessed about half the room.
The real answer?
About one in four.
Scientists call this the spotlight effect.
You feel like a bright light follows you everywhere.
But everyone else is standing in their own spotlight.
Worrying about their own shirt.
Their own stairs.
Their own two in the morning.
So that moment you're replaying tonight?
Most people never saw it.
And the ones who did already forgot.
