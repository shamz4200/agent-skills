# Night Dossier test: Wirecard cold open (60 s)

Style: Night Dossier. Voice: Shamz (Chatterbox). Ending: this is the cold open of a longer episode, so it ends on the
hook line ("This is the story of how ...").

June, twenty twenty. A small airfield outside Vienna. A man pays two pilots in cash, and boards a private jet.
He is Jan Marsalek. Until yesterday, he helped run one of Germany's biggest tech companies.
It's called Wirecard. It moves money for shops all over the world. Not long ago, it was worth more than Deutsche Bank.
But here's the strange part.
Wirecard says it has one point nine billion euros, sitting in two banks in the Philippines.
The banks say they've never heard of it.
The money isn't lost. It was never there.
By the time there's a warrant for his arrest, the jet has landed in Belarus. And Jan Marsalek is gone.
Years later, prosecutors will tell a British court who he was really working for. Russian intelligence.
This is the story of how a company invented one point nine billion euros, and how the man who knew everything vanished.

[SRC] Marsalek suspended 18 June 2020, dismissed 22 June; €1.9bn "probably never existed" (Wirecard statement, 22 June 2020): Reuters timeline.
[SRC] Two Philippine banks (BDO, BPI) said Wirecard was not a client and the documents were false: Reuters, June 2020.
[SRC] Wirecard joined the DAX in Sept 2018, valued above Deutsche Bank: Reuters / FT.
