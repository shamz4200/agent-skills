# The Shamz Story Formula (owner, Nov 2026: applies to every episode from now on, any style)

Every video is **cinematic storytelling** built to be shared: it hooks fast, makes the viewer *feel* something, gives
real value, keeps its promise, and leaves a **positive** feeling tied to Shamz, so people come back and subscribe.
Read this before writing any script. It sits on top of the style rules in `docs/STYLES.md`.

**Length (owner, Nov 2026): every full episode runs 8 to 30 minutes.** At Shamz's real pace of about 160 words a minute that is about 1,300 to 4,800 words of narration. Fill the time with story, not padding: more scenes and "how would this feel" moments, more real research beats, a re-hook every 60-90 seconds. Shorts and 60-second tests are the only exceptions.

## 1. Pick the topic (viral, but turned into a story that lasts)

- **Start from what people are talking about right now** (news, trends, a viral clip, a new study) and spin it into an
  evergreen story: a "what if you…" night, a true story told like a film, or "the science behind the thing everyone is
  sharing". The news is the doorway; the story must still work a year later.
- **Score every idea 1–5 on six things** before choosing: hook (can the first line stop a scroll?), emotion (what will
  they *feel*?), personal ("could this happen to me?"), value (what do they learn or take away?), visual (can we show
  it in Glow/ND scenes?), facts (are there solid, citable sources?). Make the highest total; never make anything
  under 3 on facts.
- **Never exploit a fresh tragedy** or mock real victims. Real people are shown with respect; allegations stay
  allegations. Debunk viral misinformation rather than spread it.

## 2. The promise (title + thumbnail + first 15 seconds)

- **Write the title and thumbnail idea first**, before the script. The title is the promise; the video must keep it.
- **The first line puts the viewer inside the moment** ("It's three in the morning on day four…"), then raises the
  stakes and the question within 15 seconds, then says what they'll get ("Tonight we find out, hour by hour").
- A thumbnail shows **one emotion on Shamz's face** plus 2–4 big words, and matches the opening scene.

## 3. Make it personal ("how would it feel if this was you?")

- Second person by default: the viewer is the one in the vault, the one awake on day four. Use the senses: what you
  see, hear, feel in your chest, what you think at 2 a.m.
- Give the viewer **small choices** ("What would you do? Most people…") so they argue in the comments.
- Pair "you" with **a real person who lived it** (Randy Gardner, the man in the New York vault): it makes it true.

## 4. The emotional arc (every episode)

Curiosity → unease → tension → a turn (the twist, the surprising science) → relief → **warmth / hope**.
- **Re-hook every 60–90 seconds**: an open loop ("but the strangest part comes on day eleven"), a new number, a reveal.
- Peaks get music swells and a slower shot; the turn gets a moment of silence before it.
- **End positive.** Never leave the viewer scared or sad: the payoff turns fear into understanding, awe or hope.

## 5. Value and payoff

- **Answer the question the title asked**, clearly, near the end (the payoff), plus one thing they didn't expect.
- Give **one takeaway they can use** ("slow your breathing: in for four, out for six"; "sleep is when your brain
  cleans itself").
- Every number shown as it's spoken, every claim sourced (`[SRC]` lines, sources in the description).

## 6. Shamz must stick in their head (recurring motifs, every episode)

- **Shamz's look never changes** (beanie with the gold patch and black cloud, golden locs, red three-dot eyes). He is
  the "you" or the guide, and he is always on the viewer's side: curious, brave, kind, a bit funny, never mocked.
- **The cloud patch glows** when Shamz figures something out (the "insight moment"), once or twice per episode.
- **"Shamz's Rule #N"**: every episode ends with one numbered life lesson on a card, in Shamz's voice, that sums up the
  payoff (e.g. *Rule #1: Panic is the only thing in the vault that can hurt you.* *Rule #2: Sleep isn't wasted time.*).
  The number keeps counting across the channel, so viewers collect them and quote them. Track them in
  `docs/SHAMZ_RULES.md`.
- **Sign-off line, the same every time**: "Stay curious. I'll see you in the next one." over the end card, then the
  question for the comments and subscribe.
- Callbacks: mention a previous episode when it fits ("remember the vault?"), and tease the next one.

## 7. Ending checklist (complete episodes)

Payoff answered → takeaway → Shamz's Rule card → a question that is easy and personal to answer in the comments →
subscribe, with a reason ("for the next night like this") → the sign-off line. A cliffhanger episode ends on the
open question instead, and says when Part 2 comes.

## 8. Workflow additions (on top of each style's pipeline)

1. Research the trend and the facts first; score ideas (section 1); tell the owner the ending (complete/cliffhanger).
2. Write 3 titles and the thumbnail idea; pick the strongest promise.
3. Write a **beat sheet** before the script: hook, stakes, the night/days timeline, 2–3 re-hooks, the turn, payoff,
   takeaway, Rule, ending. Mark the emotion of each beat.
4. Script in short spoken lines; read it aloud for rhythm; cut anything that doesn't move the story or the feeling.
5. Plates: generate early in the day (the Cloudflare free allowance resets at midnight UTC and runs out after about
   two episodes' worth); reuse the plate library when it runs out.
6. Storyboard → fix → render → 1 fps QC (feet, scale, edges, captions off faces) → master at about −14 LUFS.
7. Shorts: cut from the hook and the most emotional or surprising moment, each with its own mini promise and payoff.
8. Upload pack: title + alternates, description with sources and chapters, tags, a pinned comment that asks the
   episode's question, end screen pointing to a related episode.
9. After release: when the owner shares retention graphs, note where viewers drop and fix that kind of beat next time.
